What the baseline says

The before picture, read straight off the responses. Nothing here is modelled, nothing is drafted, and no sentence has been written for you — a baseline is worth having because it is arithmetic, and arithmetic is what this page does.

The domains

One score per person per domain, then the average of those — so somebody who answered two items counts once, not twice. Reversed items are turned round first, so every number on this page runs the same way: 5 is the favourable end.

The counted part

Minutes and headcounts are reported as a median rather than a mean: one person who spends four hours preparing would otherwise move the average for everybody, and the median says what a typical person does.

These are the rows worth asking every round rather than once before and once after. Three or four rounds of speakers per session plotted in order say something a single before-and-after pair cannot: whether the number moved when the change landed, or had already been drifting.

Reading the responses carefully

Three things worth knowing before anybody quotes a number off this page. None of them is a fault in the people who answered; they are properties of every survey ever run, and the instrument was built so that they can be seen rather than assumed away.
