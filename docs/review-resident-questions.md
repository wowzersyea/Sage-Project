**Q1 · BREAD_BUTTER · FOUNDATION**
A 3-year-old boy presents with 3 days of fever, right ear pain, and fussiness. He has no known drug allergies. Tympanic membrane is bulging and erythematous with loss of landmarks. He has no prior ear infections.

*What is the most appropriate first-line treatment?*

A. Amoxicillin 40 mg/kg/day divided twice daily for 10 days
B. Amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily for 10 days
C. Azithromycin 10 mg/kg once daily for 5 days
D. Observation without antibiotics for 48–72 hours
E. Amoxicillin-clavulanate 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily for 10 days

✅ Correct: B — Amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily for 10 days

> **Rationale:** High-dose amoxicillin is first-line therapy for uncomplicated acute otitis media in a child without beta-lactam allergy or recent amoxicillin exposure; this dose improves pneumococcal middle-ear coverage. Amoxicillin-clavulanate is usually reserved for recent amoxicillin, purulent conjunctivitis, or recurrence/treatment failure.

**Q2 · BREAD_BUTTER · FOUNDATION**
A 5-year-old girl presents with 4 days of fever, productive cough, and right lower lobe opacity on chest X-ray. She is fully vaccinated, has no known allergies, and appears well with O2 sat 97% on room air.

*What is the most appropriate outpatient antibiotic?*

A. Azithromycin
B. Amoxicillin
C. Levofloxacin
D. Ceftriaxone IM
E. No antibiotic — viral pneumonia likely

✅ Correct: B — Amoxicillin

> **Rationale:** In an immunized, well-appearing outpatient child with suspected typical bacterial community-acquired pneumonia, amoxicillin is the preferred narrow-spectrum agent for Streptococcus pneumoniae coverage. Azithromycin is mainly for suspected atypical pneumonia, and ceftriaxone/fluoroquinolone therapy is unnecessarily broad here.

A. 3 days total
B. 5 days total
