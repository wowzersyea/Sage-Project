## Domain: Fever & Sepsis

**Q1-MS · Tier 1**
A 3-week-old previously healthy infant presents with a rectal temperature of 38.3°C. Parents report decreased feeding. He appears non-toxic.

*What is the most appropriate next step?*

A. Reassure parents and follow up in 24 hours
B. Full sepsis evaluation (CBC, blood culture, UA/UC, LP) and empiric IV antibiotics
C. Blood culture only; observe in the ED for 4 hours
D. Urinalysis and urine culture only; treat if positive

✅ Correct: B — Full sepsis evaluation (CBC, blood culture, UA/UC, LP) and empiric IV antibiotics

> **Rationale:** Infants ≤28 days with fever require a full sepsis evaluation including LP, CBC, and blood/urine cultures due to significant risk of serious bacterial infection (SBI) — including meningitis. Empiric antibiotics (ampicillin + gentamicin ± acyclovir for HSV risk) are started pending results. A non-toxic appearance does not exclude SBI in this age group.

**Q2-MS · Tier 2**
A 6-week-old with fever undergoes workup: WBC 14.5k, bands 12% (band:total PMN ratio 0.25), UA negative, CSF: WBC 3, protein 52, glucose 48 (serum 95). Using the Rochester Criteria, what factor classifies this infant as HIGH risk?

A. WBC of 14,500
B. Band:total PMN ratio of 0.25 (>0.2)
C. CSF protein of 52 mg/dL
D. CSF:serum glucose ratio of 0.51

✅ Correct: B — Band:total PMN ratio of 0.25 (>0.2)

> **Rationale:** Rochester criteria define low-risk as band:total PMN ratio ≤0.2. A ratio >0.2 classifies as high-risk for SBI. The WBC 14,500 is borderline (original Rochester low-risk threshold: <15,000). The CSF values here are normal.

**Q3-MS · Tier 3**
A 10-day-old presents with fever. His mother was GBS-positive and received IV ampicillin 5 hours before delivery. The neonate appeared healthy at birth and was discharged at 48 hours.

*Which statement about this clinical scenario is MOST accurate?*

A. Adequate IAP (≥4h) eliminates risk of early-onset GBS; this fever is unlikely to be GBS
B. This is late-onset GBS disease (days 7–89); IAP provides no protection against late-onset disease
C. At 10 days, GBS disease is not possible; evaluation should focus on HSV
D. IAP efficacy is unrelated to timing; the full dose of ampicillin was protective

✅ Correct: B — This is late-onset GBS disease (days 7–89); IAP provides no protection against late-onset disease

> **Rationale:** GBS disease is classified as early-onset (0–6 days) or late-onset (7–89 days). IAP reduces early-onset GBS by ~80% but provides NO protection against late-onset GBS. At 10 days this is late-onset disease. Full sepsis workup is still indicated.

## Domain: Meningitis & CNS

**Q4-MS · Tier 1**
Which organism is the most common cause of bacterial meningitis in a previously healthy 8-month-old?

A. Listeria monocytogenes
B. Group B Streptococcus
C. Streptococcus pneumoniae
D. Neisseria meningitidis

✅ Correct: C — Streptococcus pneumoniae

> **Rationale:** After the neonatal period (>1 month), S. pneumoniae is the leading cause of bacterial meningitis in children.

**Q5-MS · Tier 2**
A 2-year-old with fever, neck stiffness, and altered mental status has CSF: WBC 1,800 (92% PMN), protein 210, glucose 22 (serum 108). Empiric ceftriaxone + vancomycin are started.

*Which adjunctive therapy reduces hearing loss in pediatric bacterial meningitis?*

A. IV immunoglobulin (IVIG)
B. Dexamethasone (0.15 mg/kg q6h × 4 days)
C. Mannitol infusion
D. Phenobarbital prophylaxis

✅ Correct: B — Dexamethasone (0.15 mg/kg q6h × 4 days)

> **Rationale:** Dexamethasone given before or with the first dose of antibiotics reduces hearing loss and neurological sequelae in H. influenzae type b and S. pneumoniae meningitis. Ideally started 15–30 minutes before antibiotics.

**Q6-MS · Tier 3**
A 5-year-old has 3 days of fever, progressive headache, and a focal seizure. MRI shows a ring-enhancing lesion in the right temporal lobe with surrounding edema. CSF: WBC 55 (65% lymphocytes), protein 90, glucose 50 (serum 95). Gram stain is negative.

*What is the MOST likely diagnosis and first-line treatment?*

A. Bacterial brain abscess; neurosurgical drainage + IV ceftriaxone + metronidazole
B. HSV encephalitis; IV acyclovir 20 mg/kg q8h
C. Tuberculous meningitis; 4-drug anti-TB therapy
D. Autoimmune encephalitis; high-dose IV methylprednisolone

✅ Correct: B — HSV encephalitis; IV acyclovir 20 mg/kg q8h

> **Rationale:** HSV encephalitis classically involves temporal lobes, presenting with fever, behavioral change, focal seizures, and aphasia. CSF shows lymphocytic pleocytosis, elevated protein, and normal glucose. IV acyclovir (20 mg/kg q8h) should be started empirically while awaiting HSV PCR.

## Domain: Respiratory Infections

**Q7-MS · Tier 1**
A 7-month-old presents in January with 3 days of URI symptoms, now with audible wheezing and SpO2 94% on room air. This is his first wheezing episode.

*What is the most likely causative pathogen?*

A. Streptococcus pneumoniae
B. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
C. Influenza A
D. Mycoplasma pneumoniae

✅ Correct: B — Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)

> **Rationale:** RSV is the leading cause of bronchiolitis in infants <2 years, peaking November–March. Management is supportive.

**Q8-MS · Tier 2**
An unvaccinated 4-year-old develops paroxysmal coughing spells with a post-tussive inspiratory whoop and vomiting for 2 weeks. WBC is 22k with 78% lymphocytes.

*What is the preferred treatment and primary goal at this stage?*

A. Amoxicillin × 10 days to eliminate the organism and shorten illness duration
B. Azithromycin × 5 days primarily to reduce transmission to contacts
C. TMP-SMX × 14 days as first-line for Bordetella pertussis
D. Supportive care only; antibiotics do not benefit the paroxysmal stage

✅ Correct: B — Azithromycin × 5 days primarily to reduce transmission to contacts

> **Rationale:** During the paroxysmal phase, azithromycin eradicates nasopharyngeal carriage and reduces transmission but does NOT significantly shorten illness.

**Q9-MS · Tier 3**
A 3-year-old is admitted with pneumonia and a large pleural effusion. Thoracentesis: pH 7.08, LDH 2,100 U/L, glucose 32 mg/dL, turbid appearance. IV antibiotics are started.

*What is the next most appropriate management step?*

A. Continue antibiotics alone; repeat imaging at 48 hours
B. Add antifungal coverage for possible fungal empyema
C. Chest tube drainage or surgical decortication (VATS)
D. Bronchoscopy to identify the anatomic source

✅ Correct: C — Chest tube drainage or surgical decortication (VATS)

## Domain: Skin & Soft Tissue

**Q10-MS · Tier 1**
A 6-year-old presents with golden-crusted lesions on the face around the nose and mouth. He is afebrile with no systemic symptoms and no induration or fluctuance.

*What is the most likely diagnosis?*

A. Herpes simplex labialis
B. Non-bullous impetigo
C. Contact dermatitis
D. Eczema herpeticum

✅ Correct: B — Non-bullous impetigo

> **Rationale:** Non-bullous impetigo (S. aureus ± S. pyogenes) is the most common bacterial skin infection in children, presenting with honey-golden crusts on the face.

**Q11-MS · Tier 2**
A 10-year-old presents with a 3 cm fluctuant, erythematous abscess on the thigh. He is afebrile with no surrounding cellulitis and no systemic signs. After incision and drainage, what additional management is most supported by current evidence?

A. No antibiotics; I&D alone is sufficient for uncomplicated abscesses
B. Oral TMP-SMX for 5–7 days to reduce treatment failure and recurrence
C. IV vancomycin for 48 hours then oral step-down
D. Oral clindamycin for 14 days

✅ Correct: B — Oral TMP-SMX for 5–7 days to reduce treatment failure and recurrence

> **Rationale:** The NEJM/Talan 2017 trial demonstrated that TMP-SMX after I&D significantly reduces treatment failure and recurrence.

**Q12-MS · Tier 3**
An 8-year-old develops rapidly spreading leg erythema after a minor scrape. Over 12 hours: severe pain disproportionate to visible findings, fever 39.8°C, skin discoloration/bullae, WBC 23k, CRP 185, lactate 3.4.

*What is the most critical immediate intervention?*

A. Start IV vancomycin + pip-tazo and admit for 48-hour IV course
B. MRI of the leg to define tissue plane involvement before deciding on surgery
C. Emergent surgical consultation for exploration and aggressive debridement
D. Emergent fasciotomy to decompress compartment syndrome

✅ Correct: C — Emergent surgical consultation for exploration and aggressive debridement

> **Rationale:** This is necrotizing fasciitis — a surgical emergency. Emergent surgical debridement is the cornerstone of treatment. Antibiotics are adjunctive. Every hour of delay increases mortality.

## Domain: Bone & Joint

**Q13-MS · Tier 1**
A 4-year-old presents with 2 days of fever and refusal to bear weight on the right leg. He holds his right hip flexed, abducted, and externally rotated. ESR 48, CRP 44, WBC 13.2k.

*What is the most likely diagnosis?*

A. Transient synovitis (toxic synovitis)
B. Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease
C. Septic arthritis of the hip
D. Osteomyelitis of the proximal femur

✅ Correct: C — Septic arthritis of the hip

> **Rationale:** Septic arthritis of the hip presents with fever, refusal to bear weight, and the hip held in the position of maximum capsular volume. This child meets 4 Kocher criteria — very high probability of septic arthritis.

**Q15-MS · Tier 3**
A 14-month-old has knee swelling, fever, and fussiness. ESR 30, CRP 20, WBC 11k. Synovial fluid WBC: 38,000. Gram stain and standard cultures are negative at 72 hours.

*What organism should be strongly suspected and how is it best detected?*

A. Kingella kingae; inoculate synovial fluid into BACTEC blood culture bottles
B. Borrelia burgdorferi; Lyme serology (ELISA + confirmatory Western blot)
C. Mycobacterium tuberculosis; synovial biopsy with AFB stain and culture

**Q17-MS · Tier 2**
A 6-month-old male is diagnosed with his first febrile UTI. After completing antibiotics, what is the most appropriate next step per AAP guidelines?

A. No further workup is needed after a first UTI
B. Renal and bladder ultrasound
C. Voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG) immediately
D. DMSA renal scan within 72 hours

✅ Correct: B — Renal and bladder ultrasound

> **Rationale:** Per 2011 AAP guidelines, renal and bladder ultrasound is recommended after the first febrile UTI in children 2–24 months. VCUG is reserved for abnormal ultrasound or recurrent febrile UTIs.

**Q18-MS · Tier 3**
A 5-year-old with known grade III VUR is on TMP-SMX prophylaxis. He develops a febrile UTI; urine culture grows E. coli resistant to TMP-SMX. After completing IV ceftriaxone, what is the highest-priority next step?

A. Switch prophylaxis to nitrofurantoin given breakthrough UTI on TMP-SMX
B. Discontinue prophylaxis; evidence for benefit with grade III VUR is insufficient
C. Pursue ureteral reimplantation given breakthrough UTI on prophylaxis
D. DMSA scan in ~6 months to assess for new renal cortical scarring

✅ Correct: D — DMSA scan in ~6 months to assess for new renal cortical scarring

A healthy neonate is born at 39 weeks to a GBS-positive mother who received IV ampicillin starting 2 hours before delivery. The infant is vigorous with APGAR 8/9.

## Domain: Vaccines & Prevention

**Q22-MS · Tier 1**
Which of the following vaccines is a LIVE attenuated vaccine?

A. Hepatitis B vaccine (HepB)
B. Inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV)
C. Measles-Mumps-Rubella (MMR)
D. Meningococcal conjugate vaccine (MenACWY)

✅ Correct: C — Measles-Mumps-Rubella (MMR)

> **Rationale:** Live attenuated vaccines in the US schedule: MMR, MMRV, varicella, intranasal influenza (LAIV), oral rotavirus, yellow fever, and oral typhoid. Live vaccines are contraindicated in severely immunocompromised patients and during pregnancy.

**Q23-MS · Tier 2**
A 14-month-old received IVIG (2 g/kg) for Kawasaki disease 4 weeks ago. He is now due for MMR and varicella vaccines.

*What is the most appropriate action?*

A. Give both vaccines now; 4 weeks is sufficient waiting time
B. Give MMR now; defer varicella only for 11 months
C. Defer both MMR and varicella for approximately 11 months from IVIG administration
D. Give both vaccines now and recheck titers in 3 months to confirm immunogenicity

✅ Correct: C — Defer both MMR and varicella for approximately 11 months from IVIG administration

> **Rationale:** IVIG contains passively acquired antibodies that interfere with response to live attenuated vaccines. For Kawasaki disease dosing (2 g/kg), defer 11 months per Red Book (AAP) recommendations.

**Q24-MS · Tier 3**
An unvaccinated susceptible 17-year-old is exposed to a household contact confirmed to have varicella 4 days ago. She is healthy and not immunocompromised.

*What is the most appropriate post-exposure management?*

A. VariZIG (varicella-zoster immune globulin) within 10 days of exposure
B. Prophylactic oral acyclovir × 7 days starting immediately
C. Varicella vaccine as soon as possible (within 5 days of exposure)
D. No intervention; varicella is self-limited in healthy adolescents

✅ Correct: C — Varicella vaccine as soon as possible (within 5 days of exposure)

> **Rationale:** For healthy, immunocompetent individuals without prior varicella immunity, varicella vaccine given within 3–5 days of exposure can prevent or attenuate disease in 70–90% of cases. VariZIG is reserved for high-risk individuals who cannot receive live vaccine.

## Domain: Antimicrobial Principles

**Q25-MS · Tier 1**
Which antibiotic class exerts its bactericidal effect by inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis through binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs)?

A. Aminoglycosides
B. Fluoroquinolones
C. Beta-lactams
D. Macrolides

✅ Correct: C — Beta-lactams

> **Rationale:** Beta-lactams covalently bind PBPs, blocking peptidoglycan cross-linking. Aminoglycosides inhibit protein synthesis at 30S. Fluoroquinolones inhibit DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. Macrolides inhibit protein synthesis at 50S.

**Q26-MS · Tier 2**
A child with recurrent S. aureus infections has MRSA confirmed.

*What is the primary mechanism conferring methicillin resistance in MRSA?*

A. Production of a beta-lactamase enzyme that directly hydrolyzes methicillin
B. Acquisition of the mecA gene encoding PBP2a — an altered PBP with low beta-lactam affinity
C. Upregulation of drug efflux pumps that export methicillin from the cell
D. Point mutations in the original PBP1–4 reducing methicillin binding affinity

✅ Correct: B — Acquisition of the mecA gene encoding PBP2a — an altered PBP with low beta-lactam affinity

> **Rationale:** MRSA resistance is mediated by mecA (carried on SCCmec), which encodes PBP2a — an altered PBP with low affinity for essentially all beta-lactam antibiotics. PBP2a cannot be overcome by any standard beta-lactam.

**Q27-MS · Tier 3**
A 12-year-old with gram-negative bacteremia is receiving once-daily gentamicin. Drug levels: peak 10 mcg/mL, trough 0.3 mcg/mL.

*Which PK/PD principle best describes aminoglycoside bactericidal activity and justifies once-daily dosing?*

A. Time-dependent killing: efficacy correlates with % time drug concentration exceeds the MIC
B. Concentration-dependent killing: bactericidal activity correlates with peak:MIC ratio
C. AUC/MIC-dependent killing: efficacy correlates with total drug exposure over 24 hours
D. Paradoxical effect: aminoglycosides lose potency at high concentrations (Eagle effect)

✅ Correct: B — Concentration-dependent killing: bactericidal activity correlates with peak:MIC ratio

> **Rationale:** Aminoglycosides demonstrate concentration-dependent killing: bactericidal activity correlates with peak:MIC ratio (target ≥8–10:1). This justifies once-daily dosing — achieves higher peaks while allowing drug-free troughs that reduce nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity.

## Domain: Immunocompromised Host

**Q28-MS · Tier 1**
An HIV-positive 4-year-old has a CD4 count of 180 cells/mm³.

*Which prophylactic medication should be initiated?*

A. Fluconazole for Candida prevention
B. TMP-SMX for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) prevention
C. Azithromycin for Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) prevention
D. Valganciclovir for CMV prevention

✅ Correct: B — TMP-SMX for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) prevention

> **Rationale:** TMP-SMX is indicated for PCP prophylaxis. Ages 1–5 years: prophylaxis when CD4 <500 cells/mm³ or CD4% <15%. MAC prophylaxis (azithromycin) is added when CD4 <50.

**Q29-MS · Tier 2**
A 7-year-old with ALL is on induction chemotherapy. On day 10 she develops fever (38.5°C) and her ANC is 180 cells/mm³. She has a central venous catheter.

*What is the most appropriate immediate management?*

A. Oral antibiotics and outpatient follow-up if she appears well
B. Blood cultures (peripheral + central line), then immediate empiric IV broad-spectrum antibiotics
C. Blood cultures and CXR; wait 24 hours for preliminary culture data before starting antibiotics
D. Empiric antifungal therapy alone given likely fungal etiology after 10 days of chemotherapy

✅ Correct: B — Blood cultures (peripheral + central line), then immediate empiric IV broad-spectrum antibiotics

> **Rationale:** Febrile neutropenia is a medical emergency. Draw blood cultures from ALL lumens AND a peripheral stick simultaneously, then start empiric broad-spectrum IV antibiotics immediately. Never wait for culture results.

**Q30-MS · Tier 3**
A 10-year-old is 45 days post-allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant (on tacrolimus for GVHD prophylaxis). He develops fever and new pulmonary nodules on CT with a halo sign. Serum galactomannan is positive (index 3.2).

*What is the most likely diagnosis and first-line treatment?*

A. Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP); IV TMP-SMX 15–20 mg/kg/day
B. Invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IPA); voriconazole
C. Disseminated cryptococcosis; amphotericin B + flucytosine
D. CMV pneumonitis; IV ganciclovir + IVIG

✅ Correct: B — Invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IPA); voriconazole

> **Rationale:** Invasive pulmonary aspergillosis — pulmonary nodules with halo sign + positive galactomannan in an allogeneic HSCT recipient on calcineurin inhibitor therapy. Voriconazole is first-line treatment (superior to amphotericin B in the landmark Herbrecht trial 2002).
