## Domain: Cervical Lymphadenitis

**Q1-AM · Tier 1 · GRID (select all that apply)**
Acute bacterial cervical lymphadenitis in a child is most often caused by S. aureus and S. pyogenes. Which agents provide reliable empiric coverage for BOTH of these pathogens (including MRSA where relevant)?

- Cephalexin
- Clindamycin
- TMP-SMX
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate
- Metronidazole
- Linezolid

✅ Clindamycin
✅ Amoxicillin-clavulanate
✅ Linezolid

> **Rationale:** Clindamycin covers MSSA, most CA-MRSA, S. pyogenes, and oral anaerobes. Amox-clav covers MSSA and Strep (plus oral anaerobes) but NOT MRSA. Linezolid covers MRSA and Strep. TMP-SMX covers MRSA/MSSA but has unreliable group A Strep coverage — a classic trap. Metronidazole has no aerobic gram-positive activity.

---

**Q2-AM · Tier 2 · MCQ**
A 4-year-old has acute unilateral cervical lymphadenitis, is well-appearing and afebrile, with no fluctuance and no MRSA risk factors. Local CA-MRSA prevalence is low.

*What is the most appropriate first-line ORAL empiric therapy?*

A. Cephalexin
B. TMP-SMX
C. Metronidazole
D. Vancomycin IV

✅ Correct: A — Cephalexin

> **Rationale:** For non-purulent acute lymphadenitis in a well child with low MRSA risk, cephalexin reliably covers MSSA and S. pyogenes — the most likely pathogens. TMP-SMX is avoided as monotherapy because of unreliable group A Strep coverage.

---

**Q3-AM · Tier 3 · FREE TEXT**
You are choosing between ampicillin-sulbactam and clindamycin for a child being admitted with cervical lymphadenitis.

*Walk through how you would decide between them — address spectrum (MSSA/MRSA/Strep/anaerobes), the likely source, and any resistance considerations.*

> **Scoring notes (free-text only):** Key concepts: Amp-sulbactam covers MSSA/Strep + oral anaerobes but NOT MRSA. Clindamycin covers MSSA, most CA-MRSA, Strep, and anaerobes. Choose clindamycin if MRSA is a concern. Amp-sulbactam favored for odontogenic/anaerobic source with low MRSA risk. Clindamycin requires D-test for inducible resistance check.

> **Model answer:** Both cover MSSA, S. pyogenes, and oral/dental anaerobes. Deciding factors are MRSA risk and source. Amp-sulbactam does NOT cover MRSA; clindamycin covers most CA-MRSA. If the child has MRSA risk factors choose clindamycin. If the picture is odontogenic/anaerobic with low MRSA risk, amp-sulbactam is an excellent narrow choice. Before relying on clindamycin for MRSA, check local resistance rates and confirm a negative D-test.

## Domain: Cellulitis & Abscess

**Q4-AM · Tier 1 · GRID (select all that apply)**
Which of the following agents provide reliable activity against MRSA?

- Vancomycin
- Cefazolin
- TMP-SMX
- Ceftriaxone
- Linezolid
- Daptomycin
- Doxycycline
- Clindamycin

✅ Vancomycin
✅ TMP-SMX
✅ Linezolid
✅ Daptomycin
✅ Doxycycline
✅ Clindamycin

> **Rationale:** MRSA-active agents: vancomycin, TMP-SMX, linezolid, daptomycin, doxycycline, and clindamycin (CA-MRSA, pending D-test). Cefazolin and ceftriaxone are beta-lactams with NO MRSA activity via mecA/PBP2a.

---

**Q5-AM · Tier 2 · MCQ**
A healthy adolescent has NON-purulent cellulitis of the lower leg — diffuse erythema, warmth, no abscess or drainage, no systemic toxicity.

*What is the best empiric oral therapy?*

A. Cephalexin
B. TMP-SMX
C. Metronidazole
D. Ciprofloxacin

✅ Correct: A — Cephalexin

> **Rationale:** Non-purulent cellulitis is predominantly caused by beta-hemolytic streptococci, so a beta-lactam (cephalexin) is first-line — routine MRSA coverage is NOT required.

---

**Q6-AM · Tier 3 · FREE TEXT**
A blood culture grows MSSA. A colleague wants to "just keep the patient on vancomycin since it covers it."

*Explain why an anti-staphylococcal beta-lactam (e.g., cefazolin or nafcillin) is preferred over vancomycin for MSSA.*

> **Scoring notes (free-text only):** Key concepts: Vancomycin is inferior to beta-lactams for MSSA. Beta-lactams are more rapidly bactericidal. Higher mortality/treatment failure/persistent bacteremia with vancomycin for MSSA. Cefazolin/nafcillin are the definitive agents once MSSA is confirmed.

> **Model answer:** Although vancomycin is technically active against MSSA, it is inferior to anti-staphylococcal beta-lactams. Cefazolin and nafcillin are more rapidly bactericidal, and multiple studies show vancomycin for MSSA bacteremia is associated with higher rates of treatment failure, persistent bacteremia, and mortality. Once susceptibility confirms MSSA, the correct move is to de-escalate.

## Domain: Intra-abdominal Infection

**Q7-AM · Tier 1 · GRID (select all that apply)**
Which agents provide reliable activity against Bacteroides fragilis and other gut anaerobes?

- Metronidazole
- Piperacillin-tazobactam
- Ampicillin-sulbactam
- Ceftriaxone
- Meropenem
- Ertapenem
- Cefepime
- Aztreonam

✅ Metronidazole
✅ Piperacillin-tazobactam
✅ Ampicillin-sulbactam
✅ Meropenem
✅ Ertapenem

> **Rationale:** Reliable anaerobic coverage: metronidazole, beta-lactam/BLI combos (pip-tazo, amp-sulbactam), and carbapenems (meropenem AND ertapenem). NO meaningful anaerobic coverage: ceftriaxone, cefepime, and aztreonam.

---

**Q8-AM · Tier 2 · MCQ**
A child with perforated appendicitis is started on ceftriaxone.

*What must be added to complete appropriate empiric coverage, and why?*

A. Metronidazole — ceftriaxone lacks anaerobic (B. fragilis) coverage
B. Vancomycin — to cover MRSA
C. Azithromycin — to cover atypicals
D. Nothing — ceftriaxone monotherapy is sufficient

✅ Correct: A — Metronidazole — ceftriaxone lacks anaerobic (B. fragilis) coverage

> **Rationale:** Intra-abdominal infections are polymicrobial (enteric gram-negatives + anaerobes). Ceftriaxone covers gram-negatives but has no reliable B. fragilis/anaerobic activity, so metronidazole is added.

---

**Q9-AM · Tier 3 · FREE TEXT**
For a perforated appendicitis, piperacillin-tazobactam monotherapy and ceftriaxone + metronidazole both provide appropriate coverage.

*How would you decide between them?*

> **Scoring notes (free-text only):** Key concepts: Both cover enteric GN + anaerobes. Pip-tazo adds Pseudomonas/Enterococcus coverage. Ceftriaxone+metro is narrower / better stewardship for community cases. Reserve pip-tazo for healthcare-associated/sicker/Pseudomonas risk.

> **Model answer:** Both regimens cover enteric gram-negatives and anaerobes. Pip-tazo additionally covers Pseudomonas and Enterococcus. From a stewardship standpoint, ceftriaxone + metronidazole is the narrower choice and is preferred for community-acquired disease where Pseudomonas is unlikely. Reserve pip-tazo for healthcare-associated infection, critically ill patients, or those with Pseudomonas risk factors.

## Domain: Febrile Neutropenia

**Q10-AM · Tier 1 · GRID (select all that apply)**
Empiric therapy for febrile neutropenia must cover Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Which of the following are reliably anti-pseudomonal?

- Cefepime
- Ceftriaxone
- Piperacillin-tazobactam
- Meropenem
- Ertapenem
- Ceftazidime
- Ciprofloxacin
- Ampicillin-sulbactam

✅ Cefepime
✅ Piperacillin-tazobactam
✅ Meropenem
✅ Ceftazidime
✅ Ciprofloxacin

> **Rationale:** Anti-pseudomonal: cefepime, pip-tazo, meropenem, ceftazidime, and ciprofloxacin. NOT anti-pseudomonal: ceftriaxone, ertapenem (the one carbapenem with no Pseudomonas coverage — high-yield trap), and amp-sulbactam.

---

**Q11-AM · Tier 2 · MCQ**
A patient with febrile neutropenia is hemodynamically stable with no localizing signs, no mucositis, no catheter-site infection, and no known MRSA. After starting cefepime monotherapy, when is it appropriate to ADD vancomycin?

A. Only for specific indications: hemodynamic instability, suspected catheter-related infection, skin/soft-tissue infection, severe mucositis, MRSA colonization, or pneumonia
B. Always — all febrile neutropenia requires empiric vancomycin
C. Never — vancomycin has no role in febrile neutropenia
D. Only if blood cultures are already positive for gram-positives

✅ Correct: A — Only for specific indications: hemodynamic instability, suspected catheter-related infection, skin/soft-tissue infection, severe mucositis, MRSA colonization, or pneumonia

> **Rationale:** Routine empiric vancomycin is NOT recommended in febrile neutropenia. It is reserved for specific indications. Adding it reflexively drives resistance and toxicity.

---

**Q12-AM · Tier 3 · FREE TEXT**
A trainee selects ertapenem for empiric febrile neutropenia coverage, reasoning that "carbapenems are broad."

*Explain the error.*

> **Scoring notes (free-text only):** Key concepts: Ertapenem does NOT cover Pseudomonas. Febrile neutropenia mandates anti-pseudomonal coverage. Use meropenem/imipenem, cefepime, ceftazidime, or pip-tazo instead.

> **Model answer:** Ertapenem is the one carbapenem that does NOT cover Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Febrile neutropenia requires reliable anti-pseudomonal coverage, so ertapenem is inappropriate as empiric monotherapy. The correct anti-pseudomonal carbapenems are meropenem or imipenem; alternatives include cefepime, ceftazidime, or piperacillin-tazobactam.

## Domain: Aspiration & Lung Abscess

**Q13-AM · Tier 1 · MCQ**
A patient develops a community-acquired aspiration pneumonia with a developing lung abscess.

*Which single agent provides appropriate coverage for the oral flora and anaerobes involved?*

A. Ampicillin-sulbactam
B. Ceftriaxone
C. Aztreonam
D. Ciprofloxacin

✅ Correct: A — Ampicillin-sulbactam

> **Rationale:** Aspiration pneumonia/lung abscess involves oral streptococci and anaerobes. Ampicillin-sulbactam (or clindamycin) covers this well. Ceftriaxone lacks reliable anaerobic coverage; aztreonam covers only aerobic gram-negatives; ciprofloxacin has poor anaerobic and unreliable streptococcal coverage.

---

**Q14-AM · Tier 3 · FREE TEXT**
Why would aztreonam be a poor choice for a patient with aspiration pneumonia?

*Be specific about what aztreonam does and does not cover.*

> **Scoring notes (free-text only):** Key concepts: Aztreonam covers only aerobic gram-negatives (incl. Pseudomonas). No gram-positive coverage. No anaerobic coverage. Aspiration needs gram-positive + anaerobe coverage.

> **Model answer:** Aztreonam is a monobactam that covers only aerobic gram-negative organisms. It has NO gram-positive and NO anaerobic activity. Aspiration pneumonia is driven by oral streptococci and anaerobes — precisely the organisms aztreonam misses. Appropriate choices are amp-sulbactam or clindamycin.

## Domain: Osteomyelitis

**Q15-AM · Tier 1 · MCQ**
Acute hematogenous osteomyelitis in children is most commonly caused by which organism?

A. Staphylococcus aureus
B. Pseudomonas aeruginosa
C. Escherichia coli
D. Bacteroides fragilis

✅ Correct: A — Staphylococcus aureus

> **Rationale:** S. aureus is by far the most common cause of pediatric osteomyelitis. Empiric therapy is directed at S. aureus, with MRSA coverage added based on local prevalence and severity.

---

**Q16-AM · Tier 2 · MCQ**
A child with osteomyelitis is improving and ready for oral step-down. The isolate is susceptible.

*Which oral agent offers excellent bioavailability and bone penetration for S. aureus?*

A. Clindamycin
B. Penicillin V
C. Nitrofurantoin
D. Azithromycin

✅ Correct: A — Clindamycin

> **Rationale:** Clindamycin has excellent oral bioavailability and bone penetration and is a workhorse for oral step-down of susceptible staphylococcal osteomyelitis (confirm D-test for MRSA). Nitrofurantoin only achieves therapeutic levels in urine.

---

**Q17-AM · Tier 3 · FREE TEXT**
For confirmed MSSA osteomyelitis, why is cefazolin or nafcillin preferred over vancomycin — and what makes clindamycin or linezolid attractive for the oral phase?

> **Scoring notes (free-text only):** Key concepts: Beta-lactams more bactericidal/better outcomes for MSSA. Vancomycin inferior for MSSA. Clindamycin/linezolid: excellent oral bioavailability & bone penetration. Confirm susceptibility/D-test before clindamycin.

> **Model answer:** For MSSA, anti-staphylococcal beta-lactams (cefazolin, nafcillin) are more rapidly bactericidal and produce better outcomes than vancomycin. For the oral phase, clindamycin and linezolid both have excellent oral bioavailability and good bone penetration — with clindamycin requiring a negative D-test to exclude inducible resistance.

Select ALL agents from this list that have reliable anti-pseudomonal activity.

- Piperacillin-tazobactam
- Cefepime
- Ceftazidime
- Ceftriaxone
- Meropenem
- Ertapenem
- Ciprofloxacin
- Aztreonam
- Tobramycin/gentamicin
- Ampicillin-sulbactam

✅ Piperacillin-tazobactam
✅ Cefepime
✅ Ceftazidime
✅ Meropenem
✅ Ciprofloxacin
✅ Aztreonam
✅ Tobramycin/gentamicin

> **Rationale:** Anti-pseudomonal: pip-tazo, cefepime, ceftazidime, meropenem, ciprofloxacin, aztreonam, and aminoglycosides. NOT anti-pseudomonal: ceftriaxone (3rd-gen but no Pseudomonas), ertapenem (the non-pseudomonal carbapenem), and amp-sulbactam.
